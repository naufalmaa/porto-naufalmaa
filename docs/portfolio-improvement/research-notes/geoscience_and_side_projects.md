# Geoscience-to-Data-Science Portfolios and "Beyond Work" / Side-Projects Sections (2024-2026)

## Which skills and keywords are in demand for "geoscience data scientist" / "subsurface data scientist" roles in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Employers want a general-purpose data stack (Python, Git, SQL/PostGIS, cloud ML services, scikit-learn/PyTorch) applied to subsurface data with domain vocabulary such as well logs, petrophysics, log normalisation/compositing, geological databases, block models and resource estimation, GIS, and OSDU. Indonesian mining roles focus on geological database management (SQL Server, ArcGIS, Python) and lead DS roles in mining SaaS.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A geoscientist moving into DS should show a **domain term next to the matching engineering term**. Examples: "LAS well-log QC → reproducible Python pipeline", "drillhole/assay DB → SQL validation rules", "lithology prediction → blind-well validation", "GIS → GeoPandas/PostGIS".
- Data quality and data management (normalisation, compositing, database consolidation, governance) come up as often as modelling. Data-engineering-flavoured projects are a good fit, not just classifiers.
- For Indonesia, nickel laterite, resource estimation and block-model data, and geological database work are the most relevant hooks. Offshore Indonesia petrophysics is also a credible hook.

## Which public geoscience datasets and community venues are respected for showcasing work?

### Takeaway
The standard well-log ML showcase datasets are FORCE 2020 (CC BY 4.0, 118 Norwegian wells, with a widely cited competition) and Equinor Volve (big and realistic, with a licence history to note). Software Underground's TRANSFORM event, Agile Scientific, and practitioner newsletters such as Andy McDonald's are the recognised community venues.

### Cited Findings
- **FORCE 2020 well log and lithofacies dataset.** Well logs from 118 wells in the Norwegian Sea, lightly cleaned and partly despiked, with lithology hand-interpreted by geoscientists. Includes LAS train/test/blind/hidden splits, a lithology scoring-matrix cost function, NPD casing depths and lithostratigraphy. Licence **CC BY 4.0** (source data under NOLD 2.0). Citation: Bormann et al. 2020, doi:10.5281/zenodo.4351156. The repo is github.com/bolgebrygg/Force-2020-Machine-Learning-competition. — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4351156)
- **FORCE 2020 competition results.** 329 teams signed up, 148 submitted, and 2,200 solutions were scored against a 10-well blind set. The winner was Olawale Ibrahim, an applied geophysics student in Lagos. Blind scores were "significantly lower" than test scores because of overfitting and a different lithology distribution, and no team scored much above 80% on the blind set. — [Sodir (Norwegian Offshore Directorate)](https://sodir.no/en/force/Previous-events/2020/results-of-the-FORCE-2020-lithology-competition)
- **Equinor Volve.** A large 2018 data release from a decommissioned field. Licence history: no licence at first, then CC BY-NC-SA in 2018, then a modified CC BY ("the Equinor licence") in 2020. Agile Scientific (Matt Hall) argued in May 2021 that it is not truly open and asked for unmodified CC BY 4.0. — [Agile Scientific](https://agilescientific.com/blog/2021/5/20/equinor-should-change-its-open-data-licence); see also [Equinor data sharing](https://www.equinor.com/energy/data-sharing)
- Andy McDonald's list of public geoscience ML datasets: **Volve** (~40,000 files, 10 years, registration required), **NLOG** (Netherlands; logs, seismic, production), **XEEK/FORCE 2020**, **NOPIMS** (Australia), **COSTA model** (Heriot-Watt, carbonates), **Kansas Geological Survey** (LAS, core images, tops), the **Norwegian core database** (1,240 wells), the **NAM Groningen** Petrel model, **Data Underground** (directory), **IODP**, and **SEG Wiki open data**. Licences vary, so check each before reuse. — [Towards Data Science (A. McDonald)](https://towardsdatascience.com/public-datasets-for-machine-learning-in-geoscience-cf880862300a/)
- **Software Underground / TRANSFORM.** Software Underground is a not-for-profit (Mahone Bay, Nova Scotia) running a Mattermost community, a blog, events, and projects. TRANSFORM 2025 tutorials are livestreamed virtual sessions of 45 or 90 minutes, labelled beginner/intermediate/advanced. Past tutorials are on SWUNG's YouTube channel, and hackathons and workshops can be proposed. — [SWUNG T25 instructor info](https://softwareunderground.org/t25-instructor-info); [TRANSFORM site](https://transform.softwareunderground.org)
- **Example practitioner site: Andy McDonald (petrophysicist turned data/product person).** A single-page site styled as a well-log depth track, with sections labelled by depth markers ("Labs", "Writing", "Projects", "TD"). It has interactive explainer "Labs" (rock physics, LWD, logging tools, ML on well logs), a Substack ("Subsurface Syntax"), YouTube, a list of 13 open datasets, 10 publications, and GitHub notebook series (Petrophysics-and-Python, SPWLA 2021 ML workshop). It also features industry case studies, including **petrophysics and geomechanics offshore Indonesia**. — [andymcdonald.scot](http://www.andymcdonald.scot/) (redirected from [andymcdgeo.github.io](https://andymcdgeo.github.io/)); [GitHub](https://github.com/andymcdgeo); [Substack](https://andymcdonaldgeo.substack.com/p/well-log-data-outlier-detection-with-machine-learning-a19cafc5ea37)
- Typical topics for his showcase writing: kNN lithology classification, random-forest well-log prediction, handling missing log data before ML, unsupervised clustering of logs, and exploring logs with the **welly** library. — [Medium/TDS kNN](https://medium.com/data-science/k-nearest-neighbors-for-lithology-classification-from-well-logs-using-python-a13ac69367ce), [missing data](https://towardsdatascience.com/identifying-and-handling-missing-well-log-data-prior-to-machine-learning-5fa1a3d0eb73), [welly](https://towardsdatascience.com/exploring-well-log-data-using-the-welly-python-library-5e808cd3137b)
- Another geoscience DS personal site was found (John O'Donnell, GitHub Pages with a clustering project page). I did not review it. — [johnodonnell123.github.io](https://johnodonnell123.github.io/pages/page_cluster.html)

### Inferences
- A FORCE 2020 project that reports **blind-well** performance and discusses why it falls below test performance shows domain-aware ML judgement. A random train/test split on depth samples would look naive.
- When using Volve, say which licence applies. That signals data-governance awareness, which the Halliburton posting lists as desirable.
- A geology-themed site design (e.g., a depth-track metaphor) can tell the career-change story without extra text. Use it sparingly.
- Giving or attending a TRANSFORM tutorial, or contributing to SWUNG-linked open-source tools (welly, lasio etc.), is a credible community item for a "Beyond Work" section.

## What should a side-projects section include to strengthen rather than dilute a professional portfolio?

### Takeaway
A small number of finished, documented, role-relevant items (open-source contributions, writing, talks or tutorials, competitions, community roles), each with evidence links and an outcome, beats a long list. Avoid tutorial clones, dead links, and "coming soon" stubs.

### Cited Findings
- Credibility signals: a clear README with setup steps and a demo or screenshots, tests or validation notes, realistic data handling, version history, explained tradeoffs, and a measurable result. Pin repos relevant to the target role. Project bullets should state the problem, stack, your ownership, and the result. "A sparse but credible portfolio is stronger than a crowded one." — [ResumeGeni](https://resumegeni.com/blog/technical-portfolio-resume) (editorial, no data)
- Things to avoid: abandoned tutorial repos or copied course projects, broken demos or dead links, private repos with no public explanation, exposed secrets, projects unrelated to the target role, screenshots with no explanation, inflated claims, and unfinished-looking school work. Suggested count for career changers is 2–3 projects (author's opinion). Recruiters may not click links on first scan, so each item needs a one-line "why it matters." — [ResumeGeni](https://resumegeni.com/blog/technical-portfolio-resume)
- David Robinson's case for public writing: it gives practice, works as a portfolio ("almost always better than sharing nothing"), brings feedback, and builds a network (Julia Silge's blog helped her get a DS job). Suggested post types: dataset analyses, explanations of concepts you recently learned, code speed-ups, open-source release announcements, interactive apps, conference write-ups, and opinion pieces. He also suggests writing about "the simplest thing you understand that almost no one outside your field does." That is a strong hook for a geoscientist. — [R-bloggers (D. Robinson, 2017)](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2017/11/advice-to-aspiring-data-scientists-start-a-blog/)
- Practitioner example: McDonald's "community/side" layer is a newsletter (200+ readers), YouTube, open datasets list, workshop materials, publications, and interactive Labs. Each is a concrete artifact with a link, not a list of interests. — [andymcdonald.scot](http://www.andymcdonald.scot/)
- Competitions are visible and credible when results are public. The FORCE 2020 winner was a student, and the organisers named the top teams. — [Sodir](https://sodir.no/en/force/Previous-events/2020/results-of-the-FORCE-2020-lithology-competition)

### Inferences
- Recommended structure for a "Beyond Work" section on a geoscience DS portfolio. Each item gets a date, a one-line outcome, and a link:
  1. **Open source**: merged PRs or issues to geoscience Python tools, or a small personal package with a README and tests.
  2. **Writing**: 2–4 posts on domain-bridging topics (e.g., why random splits leak in well-log ML, cleaning LAS files).
  3. **Talks/teaching**: TRANSFORM tutorial or lightning talk, local university or association (IAGI/HAGI/IATMI) sessions, internal brown-bags only if they can be described publicly.
  4. **Competitions**: FORCE/Xeek/Kaggle with rank, cohort size, and a link to the code.
  5. **Community**: SWUNG Mattermost help, meetup organising, mentoring.
- Don't fill the section with placeholders. If there are fewer than about 3 real items, fold them into "Projects" or "About" rather than showing a thin section. Show "last updated" dates, and remove anything older than about 3 years unless it is significant.
- Keep the section on-theme: hobbies only if one line and humanising, never as filler cards. Respect employer confidentiality by describing company work at the level of method and outcome, not data.
